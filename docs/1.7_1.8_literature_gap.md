# SurgiFlow AI — Literature Review and Research Gap

**Kanban Tasks:** 1.7 and 1.8  
**Purpose:** Understand what previous research has done and identify what SurgiFlow AI will focus on.

## 1.7 — Review of Related Research

### Why we're reviewing these papers

Before building SurgiFlow AI, we need to understand how researchers have approached emergency patient management and operating-room scheduling.

Our project deals with two connected decisions: choosing an appropriate hospital for an emergency patient and adjusting that hospital's surgical schedule to accommodate the patient.

The papers below help us understand existing approaches, the challenges involved, and the methods we can realistically explore in our project.

### Paper 1 — Dynamic Surgery Management Under Uncertainty

**Authors:** E. Gökalp, N. Gülpınar, and X. V. Doan (2023)  
**Journal:** European Journal of Operational Research  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2022.12.018

**What the researchers studied**

The researchers examined how hospitals can adjust their operating-room schedules when unexpected events occur. These events include emergency patients arriving and surgeries taking longer than planned.

They developed a mathematical approach that allows scheduling decisions to change as new information becomes available.

**What we learned**

A hospital cannot always follow its original surgical schedule. When an emergency arrives or a procedure runs late, the hospital may need to reconsider which patients should receive surgery next.

**How this relates to SurgiFlow AI**

This supports our decision to build a simulator where emergency arrivals and surgery overruns can affect existing schedules. It also gives us an established approach to compare against when evaluating our scheduling decisions.

### Paper 2 — Large-Scale Dynamic Surgical Scheduling Under Uncertainty by Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning

**Authors:** Lixiang Zhao, Han Zhu, Min Zhang, Jiafu Tang, and Yu Wang  
**Published online:** 2024; journal issue published in 2026  
**Journal:** International Journal of Production Research  
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2024.2361449

**What the researchers studied**

This paper investigates how reinforcement learning can help hospitals respond to emergency arrivals and uncertain surgery durations.

The researchers use two levels of decision-making. One decides whether the hospital should change its current schedule, while the other determines how the surgeries should be rearranged.

They also use a simulation environment to test their approach.

**What we learned**

Reinforcement learning has already been applied to dynamic surgical scheduling. One important idea from this paper is that deciding _when_ to reschedule can be just as important as deciding _how_ to reschedule.

**How this relates to SurgiFlow AI**

This paper is especially relevant to our reinforcement-learning component. However, our initial project will use simpler, more manageable methods rather than attempting to reproduce a large hierarchical deep-RL system.

### Paper 3 — Scheduling Multiobjective Dynamic Surgery Problems via Q-Learning-Based Meta-Heuristics

**Authors:** Hui Yu, Kaizhou Gao, Naiqi Wu, Meng Chu Zhou, Ponnuthurai N. Suganthan, and Shouguang Wang
**Published online:** 2024 (journal issue: June 1, 2024)
**Journal:** IEEE Transactions on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics: Systems, Volume 54, Issue 6, pp. 3321–3333
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1109/TSMC.2024.3352522

**What the researchers studied**

The researchers investigated how to improve surgical schedules when urgent patients arrive and procedure times are uncertain.

Their approach combines optimization techniques with Q-learning to help select useful search strategies. They evaluate several objectives rather than focusing on only one measure of success.

**What we learned**

A scheduling method can improve one outcome while making another worse. For example, prioritizing an emergency patient may increase delays for patients whose surgeries were already planned.

This means we should not evaluate SurgiFlow AI using only one metric.

**How this relates to SurgiFlow AI**

This paper helps justify comparing different optimization methods and measuring waiting time, overtime, disruption to planned surgeries, and other outcomes.

### Paper 4 — Stochastic Optimization Approaches for Elective Surgery Scheduling With Downstream Capacity Constraints: Models, Challenges, and Opportunities

**Authors:** Karmel S. Shehadeh and Rema Padman
**Published online:** 2021 (journal issue: January 2022)
**Journal:** Computers & Operations Research, Volume 137, Article 105523
**Link:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2021.105523

**What the researchers studied**

This review examines how operating-room scheduling is affected by resources needed after surgery, such as recovery beds and intensive-care capacity.

A patient may be ready for surgery, but the hospital still needs to consider whether appropriate postoperative resources will be available.

**What we learned**

Operating-room availability is not the only factor that determines whether a hospital can handle a patient. Other resources may become bottlenecks.

**How this relates to SurgiFlow AI**

This provides a reason to represent hospital capacity in our simulation. We can start with simpler capacity constraints and introduce more detailed recovery-bed shortages after the basic simulator is working reliably.

### Emergency Patient Allocation and Ambulance Routing

We also need to review research on how emergency services select hospitals for patients.

This is important because SurgiFlow AI does not simply choose an operating room. It first needs to consider which hospital is appropriate for the incoming patient.

Our hospital-selection logic will initially consider:

- Travel time to the hospital.
- Whether the hospital can provide the required surgical specialty.
- Available surgical resources.
- Estimated waiting time before surgery.

We will use a fixed travel-time matrix for the first version of the simulator rather than developing a real-time ambulance-routing system.

The ambulance-allocation literature should be reviewed separately to establish which destination-selection approaches are already known and to identify a suitable baseline.

## 1.8 — Research Gap and Project Contribution

### What existing research has already established

From the papers reviewed, we can see that researchers have already explored several important parts of our project.

These include dynamic operating-room scheduling, emergency patient arrivals, uncertain surgery durations, optimization methods, and reinforcement learning.

Therefore, it would be inaccurate to present SurgiFlow AI as the first project to apply AI to emergency surgical scheduling.

### Where our project will focus

Our main interest is the relationship between hospital selection and surgical scheduling.

For example, imagine an ambulance is transporting a patient who urgently needs surgery.

Hospital A is eight minutes away, but its operating rooms are busy. Hospital B is fifteen minutes away, but it has an appropriate operating room available sooner.

Should the ambulance always choose Hospital A because it is closer?

Or could Hospital B sometimes be the better choice because the patient may receive surgery sooner?

This is the type of question we want SurgiFlow AI to investigate.

### Our proposed contribution

SurgiFlow AI will be a simulation-based project that connects emergency hospital selection with operating-room scheduling across a small network of hospitals.

We will build a virtual hospital environment where emergency patients arrive unexpectedly, surgeries may take longer than expected, and hospitals have different resources and capabilities.

Within that environment, we will compare straightforward decision-making rules with optimization and reinforcement-learning approaches.

Our goal is to understand how different decisions affect emergency patient delays, scheduled surgeries, hospital workloads, and resource utilization.

**We are not claiming to have invented these algorithms or to have created a clinically validated hospital management system.** Our contribution will be implementing and evaluating these approaches together in a controlled, reproducible simulation.

### Research questions

1. Is the nearest suitable hospital always the best destination for an emergency surgical patient, or can a farther hospital sometimes provide faster access to surgery?
2. How well do different scheduling methods handle emergency arrivals and unexpected surgery overruns?
3. Can a simple reinforcement-learning agent learn useful hospital-allocation decisions compared with rule-based approaches?
4. What happens to waiting times, overtime, and planned surgeries when emergency demand increases?
5. How do differences in hospital capacity and available specialties affect the decisions made by each method?

### How we will evaluate the project

We will run the same simulated scenarios using different decision-making methods and compare their results.

Our evaluation will focus on emergency time-to-surgery, elective patient delays, operating-room overtime, scheduling disruptions, and whether decisions respect hospital capacity and specialty requirements.

We will also consider computation time, since a scheduling method needs to make decisions quickly enough to be useful in a changing environment.

### Limitations

Our initial simulator will use synthetic patient data, simplified hospital resources, and fixed travel times.

Because of these simplifications, our findings will show how the algorithms behave in our simulated environment, not how they would necessarily perform in a real hospital.

We also recognize that this is a focused review of selected research papers, not an exhaustive review of everything published in the field.

### Final project positioning

SurgiFlow AI investigates how emergency hospital allocation and operating-room scheduling can work together in a small hospital network. By simulating emergency arrivals, uncertain surgery durations, and limited hospital resources, we aim to compare different decision-making approaches and understand their trade-offs. The project focuses on building a reliable simulation, testing the algorithms fairly, and learning which strategies perform better under different conditions.
